Junction takeovers and bending-penalised minimal paths (3 Oct 2026; SparseTrack 0.8.8 baseline)
==========================================================================================

Question: where a tube meets, touches or crosses another, the flood's reading follows the other tube. Do
bending-penalised (orientation-lifted) minimal paths choose the right branch, and does putting that into the flood
help end to end?

Answer: the right branch is the straight one, and on the tube map minimal paths choose it with or without a bending
penalty. The flood's takeovers are a claim/timing problem (what arrives at its tip, and when; obstacles), not a
path-geometry problem. A lift across older crossing tubes along a bending-penalised path (Params.flood_lift_px)
fixes part of one route (m2 g005) and changes no score on any movie: left off.

2. Offline route test (route_test.py, lifted.py; P map at the trace bin, field frame, the map blurred 1.5 px so
   routes keep to the band's middle; route exit -> apex; lifted graph: 16 grid directions, a turn of one step

   Junction decision (from the junction J, arriving in the human route's direction: which branch is cheaper over
   ~20 px): 21 of 25 junctions right for every variant, plain included; the 3 wrong for every variant are not
   junctions (the "other" branch ends 2-7 px from the human one: an exit offset or a parallel route on the same
   tube); 1 (m2 g064@349) is right for all but oriented w0. Bending widens the margin where the plain one is thin
   (m2 g005@349 21 vs 30 -> 30 vs 83 with oriented w20; g048@349 68 vs 70 -> 76 vs 141; g069@244 46 vs 52 -> 39 vs
   59). With equal-distance branch ends (route_dec.py, 6 junctions; run cut short for CPU) every variant 6/6.
   Turn angles at the junctions (angles.py): the human branch is the straighter in 25/26 (median turn 3 deg vs 67
   deg onto the other branch).

   So geometry is not what goes wrong: minimal paths on the map follow the human route and prefer the straight
   branch with or without a bending penalty; the penalty makes routes cut corners (2 px coverage 0.70 -> 0.61) and
   makes the junction choice more decisive.

4. Conclusion: the routes are right on the map and the right branch is the straight one; lengths do not move
   because the flood never claimed the right branch (its claim follows arrival at its current tip, and the right
   continuation arrives later, elsewhere, or behind another grain's disc). Takeovers are a timing/ownership problem.
   What would address them: let the flood's tube cross other grains' discs where the map shows a tube there
   (8/62 m2 full traces run through one), re-read from the exit when a tube moves (open item 2), and keep a start on
   a tube passing the rim from becoming the grain's tube (g038, m1 g065). Recommendation: keep flood_lift_px off.
