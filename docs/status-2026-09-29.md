# TubeTracker status, 29 Sep 2026

## What the prototype does now

For a pollen time-lapse movie, per grain: whether and when a tube emerges (onset bracket), and the tube's length
at every time (exit on the grain's visible edge to the apex), with growth rate, the germination curve and T50.

1. **Analyse** (`Analyze_Movie_SparseTrack.command`, one or several movies; or `sparsetrack run MOVIE`):
   `runs/sparsetrack/<movie>/analysis/` holds `grains.csv` (onset interval, final length, growth rate, the model's
   confidence), `growth.csv`, `population.png` (T50) and `growth_curves.png`, and a gallery that opens with the
   movie's result in one sentence and the model's least sure grains first. `calibration.json` (asked once) adds
   um and minutes.
2. **Check and correct** (`Review_Movie_SparseTrack.command`; `sparsetrack review MOVIE`): the labelling tool opens
   with the model's answers filled in, least sure first; Enter confirms, - and = slide a trace's apex along the
   model's route, anything else is fixed as when labelling. Exports `reviewed_grains.csv`, `reviewed_traces.csv`,
   every grain's growth curve pinned to the checked lengths, and the germination curve.
3. **Adapt to a new movie's field** (`sparsetrack adapt MOVIE`, label-free, 30-60 min): the tube network fine-tuned
   on synthetic movies drawn on that movie's own field; later runs of the movie use it. Validation: see below.

## How good it is (SparseTrack 0.6.0, tag `sparsetrack-0.6.0`)

Against one annotator's traces; length within max(2 px, 10%); onset within 2 bins. Both movies were used in
development; movie 1 ("Pollen tube movie 1 7-14-26"), labelled blind with `Label_Movie1_Heldout.command`, is the
honest test of the frozen 0.6.0.

| | lengths | length and tip | onsets | T50 (model vs annotator) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sparse movie (ld) | 69/104 (66%) | 56 | 15/26 | 7735 vs 7383 frames (~1 bin) |
| crowded movie 2 | 25/54 (46%) | 18 | 5/19 | 24752 vs 24049 frames (~2.3 bins) |
| the annotator against themself | 11/14 (79%) | 11/14 | 4/7 (ld), 4/8 (m2) | |

Reviewing least sure first reached 79% of traces within tolerance after checking 28% (ld) / 41% (m2) of them,
against 48% / 65% in random order.

## What changed on 29 Sep, and why

- **Lengths from the grain's visible edge** (`Params.exit_edge`): the annotator starts every trace where the tube
  leaves the grain's visible edge, a median 1.2 px inside the census circle on ld, a property of each grain
  (spread 2.6 px between grains, 0.75 within). The steepest radial step of the before image along the exit
  tracks it (correlation 0.80 ld, 0.62 m2): ld 62 -> 69/104.
- **Flood lengths from where the tube leaves the rim zone** (`Params.flood_from_exit`): the flood's length was its
  cumulative reach from wherever it started, sometimes beside the exit and round the rim (m2 g066 read 21 px for a
  4 px stub): m2 23 -> 25/54.
- **The cloud branch merged** (`prototypes/learned_evidence/`; docs/assessment-2026-09-24.md). Its review loop was
  reworked into `sparsetrack review`; its per-bin decoder, prefix decoder and trace-once, measured on the lab's
  movies for the first time, did worse than SparseTrack: with its own network, fused evidence and continuity, ld
  40/100 lengths and 8/28 onsets (SparseTrack 0.6.0: 69/104, 15/26). Its launchers moved to
  `prototypes/learned_evidence/launchers/`.
- **Measured and not adopted** (all paired over grains on both movies): a competing flood (every tube in view floods
  at once), the onset stub at the visible edge, a flood fallback to the change reader, per-contact and
  arrival-based exit candidates for curling tubes, exits along the tube's axis for slanted tubes, reading the
  change front along the flood's route, prediction-powered population estimates from a small random review (T50
  got worse: onset errors cancel in the population), reader agreement as confidence (AUROC ~0.47), grain-body
  brightness as a germination marker, a directional bridge letting a stalled flood jump gaps ahead of its tip (no
  change on either movie), a smoothed drift check (m2 -2 lengths), and a learned space-time front reader
  (`prototypes/kymo_reader/`: 87% of lengths on held-out synthetic movies, but below SparseTrack on both real
  movies in every configuration, even trained within the same movie).

## Scoring movie 1 once (after its blind labels exist)

Score the frozen version, from its tag, on the labels, once; do not tune anything on movie 1 afterwards.

```bash
git worktree add ../tt-0.6.0 sparsetrack-0.6.0
cd ../tt-0.6.0 && ../TubeTracker/.venv/bin/python -m sparsetrack analyze ../TubeTracker/runs/sparsetrack/m1 \
    --out ../TubeTracker/runs/sparsetrack/m1_frozen_0.6.0 --grains ../TubeTracker/benchmark/labels/m1_v1.json
cd ../TubeTracker && .venv/bin/python -m sparsetrack eval --labels benchmark/labels/m1_v1.json \
    --pred runs/sparsetrack/m1_frozen_0.6.0/predictions.json --out benchmark/reports/m1_v1_scores.md
```

## A dry run on the third movie

`sparsetrack run` and `sparsetrack review` ran end to end on movie 1 (unlabelled; 69 grains; analysis 27 min on a
heavily loaded laptop, pre-fill 12 s). But movie 1's grains drift away mid-movie and new ones land after the census:
67 of 69 grains could not be followed and were read at their first place, so the results for that movie are
unreliable. The gallery now warns when over a quarter of a movie's grains could not be followed (movie 2: 8 of 24).
Following moving grains (per-bin grain detection linked into tracks, reading a grain while it is followed and
stopping when it is lost) is the next piece of work.

## Open, in order

1. **Label movie 1 blind** (`Label_Movie1_Heldout.command`, ~30 grains): the honest score of 0.6.0, and a third
   movie's labels for the learned readers, which are limited by the gap between synthetic and real footage.
   Two cautions: its grains drift, and the labelling tool keeps a grain centred with the same drift check that
   fails there, so label it once grain following has improved (or accept grains drifting out of view); and its
   census comes from the first bins, so grains that land later (around bin 40) are not in the sample. A new
   movie with settled grains would be the cleaner held-out test; movie 1 remains the robustness test.
2. Young tubes in crowded fields (m2: 18 of 29 misses are stubs of 8 px or less, mostly read as no tube): the tube
   network does not see young, faint, light-cored bulbs. Synthetic movies with emergence bulbs and a changing grain
   body (`synth` preset v6) are being tested.
3. Tubes that hug or wrap their grain (ld: 4 grains, 9 traces) and tubes leaving it at a slant (exit at the wrong
   rim point): unsolved.
4. A learned space-time front reader (`prototypes/kymo_reader/`): better than SparseTrack on synthetic movies
   (83% vs 52% of lengths) but not on the real ones so far (synthetic only: ld 50/104, m2 13/54); fine-tuned on
   ld's labels it read m2's human routes slightly better than SparseTrack's change reader on the same routes
   (15 vs 11 of 54, 95% CI -2 to +11), still below the hybrid's 25.
