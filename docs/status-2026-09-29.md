# TubeTracker status, 29 Sep - 4 Oct 2026

## What the prototype does now

For a pollen time-lapse movie, per grain: whether and when a tube emerges (onset bracket), and the tube's length
at every time (exit on the grain's visible edge to the apex), with growth rate, the germination curve and T50.

1. **Analyse** (`Analyze_Movie_SparseTrack.command`, one or several movies; or `sparsetrack run MOVIE`):
   `runs/sparsetrack/<movie>/analysis/` holds `grains.csv` (onset interval, final length, growth rate, the model's
   confidence), `growth.csv`, `population.png` (T50) and `growth_curves.png`, and a gallery that opens with the
   movie's result in one sentence and the model's least sure grains first. `calibration.json` (asked once) adds
   um and minutes.
2. **Check and correct** (`Review_Movie_SparseTrack.command`; `sparsetrack review MOVIE`): the labelling tool opens
   with the model's answers filled in, least sure first; Enter confirms, - and = slide a trace's apex along the
   model's route, anything else is fixed as when labelling. Exports `reviewed_grains.csv`, `reviewed_traces.csv`,
   every grain's growth curve pinned to the checked lengths, and the germination curve.

## 3 Oct: looking for a breakthrough on the crowded movies (research; nothing adopted yet)

Where 0.8.8 loses (per traced length): about half of all traces are young tubes (< 15 px, tolerance 2 px), hit
19/30 ld, 11/25 m2, 10/28 m1; young misses are mostly the exit end (ld) or "no tube yet" (m1: 10 of 18). Long tubes
hit 16/17 ld but 6/11 m2 and 1/11 m1. On m1, 10 of 14 "no tube" traces come from four grains whose flood never
started properly (g005, g065, g033, g047).

- **Final route first, carried backward** (prototypes/carry_front): with the annotator's own routes, carrying them
  back with composed DIS flow and reading a monotone front on the maps gives ld 82/104 (length-and-tip 81 vs 67), m2
  32/54, m1 14/50; with 0.8.8's own routes it loses on every movie. Not adopted. Carrying an EARLIER human trace
  forward read m1 24/50 (vs 15), but as a review aid it lost on m1 (below).
- **Learned tip detector** (prototypes/tip_detector), leave one movie out: apex within 4 px for 77% / 48% / 61% of
  ld / m2 / m1 traces (raw short-interval difference 34 / 20 / 30%); young tips 32/34, 16/24, 25/27; onset AUROC
  0.91 / 0.81 / 0.89 (raw 0.57-0.70); weak on long crowded tubes. Being built into the onset and young-tube reading.
- **Per-movie network adaptation** (prototypes/tube_adapt): fine-tuning on 15 human-corrected m1 grains makes the
  maps mark 90-96% of the other grains' tubes (from 75%) and raises their onsets 8/27 -> 14-17/27 (CI above 0);
  lengths +3..+7/50 (not significant); marks beside the tubes rise (rim marks before onset do not). The flood's rules, tuned on the shipped maps, do
  not yet turn better maps into better lengths. Label-free self-training on the tracker's own confident readings (prototypes/self_train) gives the same map gain (m1 75% -> 90%, m2 90% -> 96%) but small, non-significant end-to-end gains (m1 lengths +2..+6, onsets +3..+5; seed noise +/-3); kept as an experiment.
- **Review aid** (branch of the review_fill agent, unmerged): filling a grain's curve from the image along one or two
  person's traces beats the app's rescaling on m2 (+8 lengths) but not on m1 (-3). It found that the app's current
  rescaling after one late trace makes earlier lengths worse than 0.8.8 alone on all three movies (lengths +5 / +6 /
  +4, length-and-tip +10 / +11 / +7 for leaving 0.8.8's readings): a fix is being measured.
- **Junction takeovers** (junction agent's branch): most are not a wrong choice at a junction - the right branch was
  never claimed (the tube stalls at an older crossing tube); bending-penalised paths pick the straight branch but so
  does the plain path. Params.flood_lift_px (off) carries a stalled tube across an older crossing tube along a
  bending-penalised path (m2 g005 96 -> 206 px of 257); end-to-end result pending.
- Literature scout: the closest published method for growing networks (fungal hyphae, Chassereau et al. 2024) is an
  arrival flood like ours; foundation models (SAM2, micro-SAM, point trackers) are documented failures on thin
  faint filaments.

## 2 Oct: the app, made easier to use

The analysis did not change; the window did (`tubetracker/app/`). What a person sees now:

- **The movie at a glance.** With no grain selected, the side panel shows the movie's numbers (grains counted and
  why the others are not, germinated, T50, growth, final length, lost, checked) and the checks left, with Start
  Checking. Esc comes back to it. The germinated share is the germination curve's end everywhere (it was the
  plain count ratio in the window but the curve's in Compare and the exports: 79% against 82% on movie 2).
- **Why to check, and what to do.** A grain's reasons are links to their times, with one sentence each
  ("At 84 min the model reads 3.4 px: its least sure reading of this tube (8% confidence)"). The corrections are
  grouped by what they are about: Onset (here, never), Tube (set tip, draw, none here), Grain (burst, not a grain,
  clump), under Confirm and Undo; right-click a grain for the same. The tool in hand shows as pressed, and what to
  click is written across the movie; saved corrections, and why one could not be taken, show over the movie for a
  moment. The timeline's last lane holds every grain to check (it held only the least sure lengths).
- **Framing.** A grain opens framed with its tube at its longest, in the movie and in the close-up (aimed between
  the grain and its tube, as close as shows both).
- **Plain words.** Times read "37 of 120 min" (frames when no duration is given, with a note saying how to get
  minutes), an onset within one minute as that minute, no "bins" on screen.
- **Help > TubeTracker Help (F1, ?)**: what everything on screen means, how to check and correct, how the numbers
  are defined (counted grains, onset, T50, growth, confidence), the files. Results says what its columns mean and
  which units to give; Compare keeps one time axis (movies without a duration are named, not mixed in).
- Smaller: drop a movie on the window to open it; the window reopens where it was; File > Show in Finder; a Mac
  notification when an analysis finishes while the window is in the back; sample IDs start without the movie's
  suffix (the sparse demo's was fixed).
- A separate review pass (an agent using the app as a new user would) found 20 problems; the ones that changed
  results: **Enter on a grain the model could not read counted it as "never germinated"** (sparse movie: 31
  counted / 94% became 32 / 88%), now refused with what to press instead; Compare showed the model's numbers while
  Results showed the checked ones (Compare now reads each movie as the app does). Also fixed: the side panel
  widening past the window on movies timed in frames, "Check 1 of 27" never counting on, the slider swallowing
  O/T/D/Enter after it was dragged, a 0.5 px vs 2 px disagreement on what counts as a tube, internal words
  ("network", "change region", "bin") on screen, and Help statements that did not match the code.

## 1 Oct: higher sample rates (more frames per second sampled)

The cache can now average every frame or every k-th frame instead of the keyframes only (`sparsetrack prepare
--sample all|N`, `stack.prepare(sample=...)`; the cache records its `sampling`). On the sparse movie (labelled
grains, SparseTrack 0.8.4, paired over 28 grains against keyframes at 300 frames per bin):

| sampling | bins | onsets | lengths | length and tip | T50 (annotator 7383) | analysis + maps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| keyframes, 300 frames per bin (as before) | 176 | 15/26 | 75/104 | 67 | 7559 | 55 + 35 s |
| every frame, 300 per bin | 176 | 14/26 | 70/104 (-5, CI -9 to -1) | 62 | 7999 | 55 + 36 s |
| keyframes, 150 per bin | 351 | 13/27 | 69/104 (-6, -12 to -2) | 58 | 8790 | 124 + 74 s |
| keyframes, 150 per bin, bin-counted settings doubled | 351 | 11/27 | 70/104 (-5, -10 to 0) | 63 | 7735 | 117 s |
| keyframes, 100 per bin | 526 | 12/27 | 70/104 (-5, -11 to +1) | 58 | 8526 | 229 + 119 s |

Nothing beats the keyframes at 300 frames per bin. In these x264 movies the frames between keyframes are near-copies
of the keyframe before them (mean change from it 0.02-0.1 grey levels over the next 11 frames, 0.5 to the next
keyframe; per-bin background noise 0.115 with keyframes, 0.110 with every frame), so averaging them adds no
information and slightly smears growth; smaller bins average fewer real images, and each is noisier. The labels were
made on 300-frame bins, so finer onset timing could not have shown here, but lengths and tips (compared at the same
frames) got worse. More frames per second would help only with recordings whose frames are independent (keyframes
every frame or every few frames, or lossless): then a bin averages up to 300 independent images instead of 25.

## 1 Oct: the drawn tube sat on the tube's dark edge, and stayed put while grains drifted (SparseTrack 0.8.3)

Seen in the app: tubes drawn along a dark edge rather than the clear middle, sometimes a few px off, and grains
drifting away from their marks. Two causes, both in what the analysis stored, not in the tube network or the labels:

- **Drift kept for followed grains only.** Every grain is read in its own frame (crops registered by the followed
  drift, or else by the phase track), but only grains followed beyond `track_far_r` radii kept that drift: 32 of the
  sparse movie's 36 grown grains drew their tube (and circle) where they started (local motion up to 20 px, g017).
  Both readers now keep it for every grain.
- **Routes on the darker wall.** Across a tube these movies show two dark walls about 7 px apart with a clear middle,
  one wall darker. The annotator's traces lie on the middle (sparse movie: within 1 px of the brightest point; the
  walls at -3.5 and +3.75 px), so nothing needs re-annotating; the tube network marks the whole width evenly, so
  nothing needs retraining. But the change reader's cheapest path through its change map and the flood's geodesic
  centreline both run along the darker wall: 3.5 px (median) from the traces. `learned.centre_route` moves each
  route point along its normal to the middle of the network's band across the tube (the map averaged over the last
  3 bins the grain was seen), and per bin does the same on that bin's map, kept every 5 px as offsets from the route
  (`res["bend"]`, `sparsetrack/routes.py`; tubes bend and are pushed as they grow: a route centred on the end lay 2-3
  px off traces made 100 bins earlier). The change reader's tips (points of its route) move with it; the flood's
  (its farthest tube pixel, already on the tube) stay.

## What changed for 0.7.0 (29 Sep, evening)

- **Grains are followed** (`Params.grain_track = "auto"`, `sparsetrack/track.py`): each grain is tracked by its own
  look bin by bin (a bank of templates, 8 px search per bin widening after misses, never onto a neighbour still at
  its place), refined by the phase correlation where the two agree. A grain that moves further than its diameter
  is read in its own frame; a grain that cannot be found any more (burst, swept off, out of the frame) is read to
  that bin and held from there (`grain_lost_after`; an ungerminated lost grain is censored there in the germination
  curve). Tracking: synthetic moving grains 99.4% of bins within 2 px (0.6.0 64.6%); movie 2: 108 of 122 grains
  followed to the end (0.6.0 rejected 53 of these tracks); movie 1: 41 of 69 to the end, 49 for at least half.
  End to end it costs a little on the development movies (a burst held on each; m2 g064, pushed off a tube that
  stayed put) and gains on synthetic moving grains (movie 2's field: 130 -> 154 of 339 lengths). The labelling
  tool follows grains the same way, so movie 1 can now be labelled.
- **Tip continuation** (`Params.tip_continue`, on): a tube whose front reached its path's end well before the movie
  ends and whose new material keeps arriving beyond it in order outwards (a tube turning back along its grain) is
  read on by a second front. ld 69 -> 73/104 (+4, CI +0 to +9); synthetic v5, where nothing was chosen, +10/647 (CI
  +0 to +21); m2 unchanged (flood-read).
- A cache bug found on the way: the grain-following cache was keyed by the movie array's id, which Python reuses
  once an array is freed, so a process analysing several movies could reuse another movie's drift; fixed (the
  cached entry keeps its movie and is checked by identity).

- **Lengths from the grain's visible edge** (`Params.exit_edge`): the annotator starts every trace where the tube

  tracks it (correlation 0.80 ld, 0.62 m2): ld 62 -> 69/104.

  cumulative reach from wherever it started, sometimes beside the exit and round the rim (m2 g066 read 21 px for a

- **The cloud branch merged** (`prototypes/learned_evidence/`; docs/assessment-2026-09-24.md). Its review loop was
  reworked into `sparsetrack review`; its per-bin decoder, prefix decoder and trace-once, measured on the lab's

  `prototypes/learned_evidence/launchers/`.
- **Measured and not adopted** (all paired over grains on both movies): a competing flood (every tube in view floods

## Tubes move

The annotator's traces of the same tube at successive times lie a median 1-2 px apart, but on movie 2 a quarter of
them more than 6 px and a tenth more than 17 px (g069 23 px, g038 and g092 19 px; ld: a tenth more than 7 px). Both
readers assume a grown tube stays where it grew. A first reader that follows the tube as a moving curve (the final
route carried backwards bin by bin by matching the tube's cross-section) was worse overall (human final routes, change
evidence: m2 18 vs 15 of 62 traces, ld 47 vs 55 of 116): it also drifts on tubes that stay put and runs past the tip.
Movie 1's grains move a median 33 px by the end, each its own way (a smooth field-wide warp does not explain it).

Dense optical flow does follow them: carrying an annotator's route to their next trace of the same tube with OpenCV's
DIS flow between 3-bin means, composed over 10-bin steps, lands it a median 1.3 px from that trace where the tube had
moved (left in place: 9.0 px) on movie 2, and 1.0 px (10.1) on the dev movie, with no drift on tubes that stayed
put; within 2 px: m2 44% -> 78% of trace pairs, ld 75% -> 96% (one tube lost, g069). Warping whole probability maps
into the last bin's frame with the same flow does not work: the flow stretches a growing tube (g092 at bin 140
showed 149 px of tube against the annotator's 60), so the flood on such maps read worse (3 vs 5 hits on 4 grains).
