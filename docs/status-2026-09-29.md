# TubeTracker status, 29 Sep 2026

## What the prototype does now

For a pollen time-lapse movie, per grain: whether and when a tube emerges (onset bracket), and the tube's length
at every time (exit on the grain's visible edge to the apex), with growth rate, the germination curve and T50.

1. **Analyse** (`Analyze_Movie_SparseTrack.command`, one or several movies; or `sparsetrack run MOVIE`):
   `runs/sparsetrack/<movie>/analysis/` holds `grains.csv` (onset interval, final length, growth rate, the model's
   confidence), `growth.csv`, `population.png` (T50) and `growth_curves.png`, and a gallery that opens with the
   movie's result in one sentence and the model's least sure grains first. `calibration.json` (asked once) adds
   um and minutes.
2. **Check and correct** (`Review_Movie_SparseTrack.command`; `sparsetrack review MOVIE`): the labelling tool opens
   with the model's answers filled in, least sure first; Enter confirms, - and = slide a trace's apex along the
   model's route, anything else is fixed as when labelling. Exports `reviewed_grains.csv`, `reviewed_traces.csv`,
   every grain's growth curve pinned to the checked lengths, and the germination curve.

## What changed for 0.7.0 (29 Sep, evening)

- **Grains are followed** (`Params.grain_track = "auto"`, `sparsetrack/track.py`): each grain is tracked by its own
  look bin by bin (a bank of templates, 8 px search per bin widening after misses, never onto a neighbour still at
  its place), refined by the phase correlation where the two agree. A grain that moves further than its diameter
  is read in its own frame; a grain that cannot be found any more (burst, swept off, out of the frame) is read to
  that bin and held from there (`grain_lost_after`; an ungerminated lost grain is censored there in the germination
  curve). Tracking: synthetic moving grains 99.4% of bins within 2 px (0.6.0 64.6%); movie 2: 108 of 122 grains
  followed to the end (0.6.0 rejected 53 of these tracks); movie 1: 41 of 69 to the end, 49 for at least half.
  End to end it costs a little on the development movies (a burst held on each; m2 g064, pushed off a tube that
  stayed put) and gains on synthetic moving grains (movie 2's field: 130 -> 154 of 339 lengths). The labelling
  tool follows grains the same way, so movie 1 can now be labelled.
- **Tip continuation** (`Params.tip_continue`, on): a tube whose front reached its path's end well before the movie
  ends and whose new material keeps arriving beyond it in order outwards (a tube turning back along its grain) is
  read on by a second front. ld 69 -> 73/104 (+4, CI +0 to +9); synthetic v5, where nothing was chosen, +10/647 (CI
  +0 to +21); m2 unchanged (flood-read).
- A cache bug found on the way: the grain-following cache was keyed by the movie array's id, which Python reuses
  once an array is freed, so a process analysing several movies could reuse another movie's drift; fixed (the
  cached entry keeps its movie and is checked by identity).

## What changed on 29 Sep, and why

- **Lengths from the grain's visible edge** (`Params.exit_edge`): the annotator starts every trace where the tube
  leaves the grain's visible edge, a median 1.2 px inside the census circle on ld, a property of each grain
  (spread 2.6 px between grains, 0.75 within). The steepest radial step of the before image along the exit
  tracks it (correlation 0.80 ld, 0.62 m2): ld 62 -> 69/104.
- **Flood lengths from where the tube leaves the rim zone** (`Params.flood_from_exit`): the flood's length was its
  cumulative reach from wherever it started, sometimes beside the exit and round the rim (m2 g066 read 21 px for a
  4 px stub): m2 23 -> 25/54.
- **The cloud branch merged** (`prototypes/learned_evidence/`; docs/assessment-2026-09-24.md). Its review loop was
  reworked into `sparsetrack review`; its per-bin decoder, prefix decoder and trace-once, measured on the lab's
  movies for the first time, did worse than SparseTrack: with its own network, fused evidence and continuity, ld
  40/100 lengths and 8/28 onsets (SparseTrack 0.6.0: 69/104, 15/26). Its launchers moved to
  `prototypes/learned_evidence/launchers/`.
- **Measured and not adopted** (all paired over grains on both movies): a competing flood (every tube in view floods
  at once), the onset stub at the visible edge, a flood fallback to the change reader, per-contact and
  arrival-based exit candidates for curling tubes, exits along the tube's axis for slanted tubes, reading the
  change front along the flood's route, prediction-powered population estimates from a small random review (T50
  got worse: onset errors cancel in the population), reader agreement as confidence (AUROC ~0.47), grain-body
  brightness as a germination marker, a directional bridge letting a stalled flood jump gaps ahead of its tip (no
  change on either movie), a smoothed drift check (m2 -2 lengths), and a learned space-time front reader
  (`prototypes/kymo_reader/`: 87% of lengths on held-out synthetic movies, but below SparseTrack on both real
  movies in every configuration, even trained within the same movie).
- **`sparsetrack adapt` removed** (label-free fine-tuning of the tube network on synthetic movies drawn on a new
  movie's own field). Validation on movie 2: the network trained without movie 2's field reads 10/54 lengths; after
  adapting it to movie 2's field (two synthetic movies, 3000 steps, 57 min) still 10/54 (onsets 6/16 -> 5/17, length
  and tip 9 -> 10). The shipped network, trained on both fields from the start, reads 25/54, so whatever it gains on
  movie 2 is not recovered by fine-tuning on a few synthetic movies. The code is in git history (commit 2ea8085).

## Tubes move

The annotator's traces of the same tube at successive times lie a median 1-2 px apart, but on movie 2 a quarter of
them more than 6 px and a tenth more than 17 px (g069 23 px, g038 and g092 19 px; ld: a tenth more than 7 px). Both
readers assume a grown tube stays where it grew. A first reader that follows the tube as a moving curve (the final
route carried backwards bin by bin by matching the tube's cross-section) was worse overall (human final routes, change
evidence: m2 18 vs 15 of 62 traces, ld 47 vs 55 of 116): it also drifts on tubes that stay put and runs past the tip.
Movie 1's grains move a median 33 px by the end, each its own way (a smooth field-wide warp does not explain it).

## Scoring movie 1 once (after its blind labels exist)

Score each frozen version, from its tag, on the labels, once; do not tune anything on movie 1 afterwards.

```bash
for v in 0.6.0 0.7.0; do
  git worktree add ../tt-$v sparsetrack-$v
  (cd ../tt-$v && ../TubeTracker/.venv/bin/python -m sparsetrack analyze ../TubeTracker/runs/sparsetrack/m1 \
      --out ../TubeTracker/runs/sparsetrack/m1_frozen_$v --grains ../TubeTracker/benchmark/labels/m1_v1.json)
  .venv/bin/python -m sparsetrack eval --labels benchmark/labels/m1_v1.json \
      --pred runs/sparsetrack/m1_frozen_$v/predictions.json --out benchmark/reports/m1_v1_scores_$v.md
done
```

## A dry run on the third movie

`sparsetrack run` and `sparsetrack review` ran end to end on movie 1 (unlabelled; 69 grains; analysis 27 min on a
heavily loaded laptop, pre-fill 12 s). But movie 1's grains drift away mid-movie and new ones land after the census:
67 of 69 grains could not be followed and were read at their first place, so the results for that movie are
unreliable. The gallery now warns when over a quarter of a movie's grains could not be followed (movie 2: 8 of 24).
Following moving grains (per-bin grain detection linked into tracks, reading a grain while it is followed and
stopping when it is lost) is the next piece of work.

   tubes move and cross others, and the flood freezes when a moved tube re-appears as a block. A tube's growing

   "before" image (see "Tubes move" above; being built into the readers).
3. Young tubes in crowded fields (movie 2: 10 misses are stubs read as no tube).
4. Tubes leaving their grain at a slant (exit at the wrong rim point): six exit rules tried, all below 0.6.0.
